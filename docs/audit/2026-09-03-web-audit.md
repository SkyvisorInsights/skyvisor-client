Working tree clean at audit time. A second worktree exists at `.superpowers/sdd/2026-07-25-trust-evidence-surface/worktree-client` on `feat/trust-surface`; it was not touched.

## 1. Summary

- **Every gate is green.** `go vet` and `golangci-lint` report zero issues, 18 Go packages pass under `-race`, and all 44 Bun tests pass across 6 files.
- **The page weight is the problem.** OpenLayers shipped on every page as a render-blocking script while only 11 of the templates need it, putting 877 KB in front of first paint on every flagship surface. Now fixed: it loads per route.
- **The app runs two map libraries.** MapLibre draws the track, globe and situation surfaces; OpenLayers draws the Reference and Explorer pages (airlines, airports, flights, locations) from inline scripts against a global `ol`. Together they are about 1.9 MB. Consolidating onto MapLibre is worth doing and is a real migration, not a deletion.
- **MapLibre is handled correctly.** Its 1 MB bundle is fetched lazily, only where a map exists.
- **Animation replay is real but cosmetic.** Three motion helpers have no ready-guard while a fourth does, so partial page swaps re-run enter and pulse animations on content that did not change.
- **No Content-Security-Policy.** Cookie flags, CSRF and framing protection are all correctly configured, which makes the missing CSP the one conspicuous gap in an otherwise careful setup.

## 4. Performance

**Correction.** An earlier draft of this audit recorded OpenLayers as unused and recommended deleting it. That was wrong. The grep behind it searched `app/assets/**/*.js`, and the usage lives in inline `<script>` blocks inside templ files, which call a global `ol`. Eleven templates draw OpenLayers maps: `AirlineMap`, `AirlineDetailedMap`, `AirportMap`, `AirportDetailedMap`, `DetailedFlightsMap`, `FlightsPreviewMap`, `LiveFlightsPreviewMap`, `CityMap`, `CityDetailedMap`, `CountryMap`, `CountryDetailedMap`. Deleting the bundle would have broken every one of them.

The real finding is narrower and still worth acting on: the bundle was loaded globally and needed on four route trees. It now loads only on `/airlines`, `/airports`, `/flights` and `/locations`, so the 877 KB is gone from first paint on the track, globe, situation, dashboard, watches, trips, pricing and onboarding surfaces. Those are the pages that carry the product's first impression and its marketing screenshots.

Adding `defer` to `arc.js` and moving the MapLibre stylesheet to map pages recovers a further ~70 KB on non-map routes. After that a non-map page carries roughly 172 KB of JavaScript, the Alpine, htmx and Motion bundle, plus about 117 KB of CSS, which is a reasonable place to stop.

The larger question is the two map libraries. MapLibre is the strategic one; OpenLayers survives only on the Reference and Explorer pages, which also still read from web-local Postgres rather than the API. Migrating those pages onto MapLibre would remove 877 KB from the repository entirely and leave one map stack to style, and it should be scoped as its own piece of work.

## 5. Accessibility

Reduced-motion handling is genuinely thorough: every animation entry point consults `reducedMotion()` before doing anything. The skip link is implemented properly. Two areas were not verifiable by static reading and should be checked in a browser: contrast of `--muted-foreground` against `--card` in dark mode, and keyboard reachability plus ARIA labelling of the MapLibre control cluster on the globe and situation pages.

## 6. Security

Cookies, CSRF and clickjacking protection are all correctly configured. The gap is CSP (W5). Because Alpine relies on inline expressions and PostHog injects a snippet, go report-only first, read the violations, then enforce.

## 7. Test gaps

Coverage is real rather than nominal, so the gaps are narrow: no test asserts that a second `htmx:afterSwap` on the same node does not re-animate it (this is the regression test for W4), and there is no automated accessibility or visual check.
